What is quishing? QR code phishing, and how to look back at a code you scanned

Quishing is phishing hidden inside a QR code. A fake code on a parking meter, a flyer or an email takes you to a look-alike login, payment or delivery page. Because you cannot read a QR code with your eyes, the trick works on careful people too. This page explains how quishing works and the warning signs, and shows how QR Code Halo opens a code the moment you scan it and keeps it in History so you can look back at it later.

How a quishing scam works

  1. A real code is swapped or a fake one is sent. A sticker goes over a parking meter, EV charger or poster code, or an email or letter asks you to scan to fix an account, a parcel or a fine.
  2. The code holds a link you cannot see. It often points to a domain that looks close to the real one, or to a short link that hides the final address.
  3. The page asks for something valuable. A password, a card number, a one-time code or a payment, usually with a short deadline.

Warning signs of a quishing code

SignWhy it matters
A sticker on top of a printed codeThe easiest way to replace a real code with a fake one
A domain that is almost rightSwapped letters, extra words or letters from another alphabet that look the same
A link that is not httpsOrdinary payment and login pages use https
Urgency plus a payment or loginPressure to act right now is the core of phishing

Frequently asked questions

What does quishing mean?

Quishing is a blend of QR and phishing. Instead of a suspicious link in a message, the scam hides the link inside a QR code, so you cannot read where it goes just by looking at it.

Where do quishing codes show up?

Common places are stickers placed over real codes on parking meters and EV chargers, flyers and posters, restaurant tables, and emails or letters that ask you to scan to fix an account, a delivery or a payment.

I already scanned a code. How can I find it again?
