What is quishing? QR code phishing explained

Quishing is phishing hidden inside a QR code. A fake code on a parking meter, a flyer or an email takes you to a look-alike login, payment or delivery page. Because you cannot read a QR code with your eyes, the trick works on careful people too. This page explains how quishing works, the warning signs, and how QR Code Halo flags a suspicious link in a code you scanned.

How a quishing scam works

  1. A real code is swapped or a fake one is sent. A sticker goes over a parking meter, EV charger or poster code, or an email or letter asks you to scan to fix an account, a parcel or a fine.
  2. The code holds a link you cannot see. It often points to a domain that looks close to the real one, or to a short link that hides the final address.
  3. The page asks for something valuable. A password, a card number, a one-time code or a payment, usually with a short deadline.

Warning signs of a quishing code

SignWhy it matters
A sticker on top of a printed codeThe easiest way to replace a real code with a fake one

Frequently asked questions

What does quishing mean?

Quishing is a blend of QR and phishing. Instead of a suspicious link in a message, the scam hides the link inside a QR code, so you cannot read where it goes just by looking at it.

Where do quishing codes show up?

Common places are stickers placed over real codes on parking meters and EV chargers, flyers and posters, restaurant tables, and emails or letters that ask you to scan to fix an account, a delivery or a payment.

I already scanned a code. How can I look at it again?

In QR Code Halo, every scan outside batch mode is saved on your device unless Skip saving to history is on. Open the code in History and tap Security Check to see the full link broken into parts, rated Safe, Caution or Blocked.

Does the Security Check send my scans to a server?

No. It parses the content on your device with fixed rules and does not do an online reputation lookup.

Will it open a javascript: or data: code?

No. javascript:, data: and file: codes are never opened.

Is scanning limited?

No. Scanning with the camera or from photos is unlimited for everyone, with no scan count.
